## Section 1: Standout Individual Portfolio Websites

### Executive and Leadership Level

These are the patterns that matter for a VP/GM/CEO level professional. The best leadership portfolios do NOT look like designer portfolios. They look like credibility engines.

**Leadership Folios Collection** (leadershipfolios.com)
A curated set of 33 annotated portfolios from UX Managers, Directors, VPs, and Executives. Cross portfolio patterns that emerged:

- **Scope clarity**: Senior leaders state team size, business impact, and accountability explicitly. Not "I designed X" but "Led 12 person team that shipped X, driving $Y revenue."
- **Consistent framing**: Repeatable structures across roles replace lengthy case studies. Each role reads like a ledger entry: title, scope, defining decision, business metric.
- **Public work**: Independent projects (blog, open source, newsletter) answer the "still hands on?" question.
- **Business metrics over design outcomes**: Revenue, user numbers, operational improvements replace pixel level detail.

Specific standouts from that collection:

| Name | Role | What Works |
|------|------|------------|
| Emily Campbell | Director of Design for AI Models, Figma | Role based organization with inline product artifacts; each position carries a leadership verb and tangible proof |
| Pavel Bukengolts | Director, UX Design Lab | Handles confidential work through consistent formatting: leadership verb + defining decision + business outcome. Six case studies with measurable results (40% faster production, 56% effort reduction) |
| Renato Valdes Olmos | VP/Founder, Noord (formerly VP Product at Pitch) | Treats each role as a ledger entry with consistent components. Demonstrates continued hands on work alongside executive roles |

Source: [Leadership Folios](https://www.leadershipfolios.com/)

## Section 3: SEO and Traffic Patterns

What the best performing portfolio sites do differently, synthesized from Wix's portfolio SEO guide, Squarespace's SEO documentation, and the Gemeos Webflow vs Framer audit.

### 1. Keyword Architecture

The top sites don't just sprinkle keywords. They map a keyword hierarchy:

- **One primary keyword** per page (e.g., "VP Product Management Toronto")
- **5 to 10 secondary service keywords** per site (e.g., "product strategy consultant," "growth stage product leader")
- **Project level keywords** reflecting what employers or clients actually search for

Geographic modifiers matter if targeting a specific market ("product leader Canada," "GM SaaS Toronto").

Source: [Wix: Portfolio SEO Guide](https://www.wix.com/blog/portfolio-seo)

### 2. Page Structure That Ranks

Homepage must include: clear H1 with primary keyword, intro paragraph describing niche, featured projects section, services overview in keyword rich language, and concise SEO friendly bio.

Each case study page needs: keyword rich H1 title, overview paragraph, challenge/approach section with service keywords, outcome description with metrics, and descriptive images with alt text.

Source: [Wix: Portfolio SEO Guide](https://www.wix.com/blog/portfolio-seo)

### 3. Technical Performance

**63% of portfolio sites fail Google's Core Web Vitals speed tests** (2026 data). The sites that pass do three things:

- WebP images instead of JPG/PNG, compressed before upload
- Minimal third party scripts (analytics, chat widgets, social embeds all hurt LCP)
- CDN delivery (Webflow and Framer both use edge networks; Super.so caches via CDN)

Webflow sites averaged 1.9s LCP vs Framer's 2.1s in the Gemeos audit of 50 sites, largely because Webflow auto compresses and minifies while Framer injects analytics scripts by default.

Source: [Gemeos: Webflow vs Framer](https://www.gemeosagency.com/en/blog/webflow-vs-framer-which-no-code-tool-to-choose)

### 4. Schema Markup

The highest ranking personal sites implement structured data:

- **Person schema** (name, jobTitle, worksFor, sameAs links to LinkedIn/GitHub)
- **CreativeWork schema** on case studies
- **BreadcrumbList** for site navigation
- **ImageObject** with proper alt text metadata

Most portfolio builders do NOT add this automatically. Webflow and Super.so allow custom code injection for schema. Squarespace has partial support. Wix has an SEO app for it.

Source: [Wix: Portfolio SEO Guide](https://www.wix.com/blog/portfolio-seo)

### 5. Content as the SEO Engine

The executive sites that dominate search (Seth Godin, Neil Patel, James Clear) all treat content publishing as their primary SEO strategy. The portfolio itself is a small part of the site; the blog/newsletter archive is the traffic magnet.

- Seth Godin: daily publishing, thousands of indexed pages
- Neil Patel: free tools + massive content library = organic traffic machine
- James Clear: research backed essays targeting specific long tail queries

For a product leader, this means: the Substack archive, case studies published as blog posts, and thought leadership pieces are more important for search traffic than the portfolio shell itself.

Source: [Searchbloom: Best Personal Branding Websites for CEOs](https://www.searchbloom.com/blog/best-personal-branding-websites-for-ceos/)

### 6. Backlink Strategy

- Directory listings (Google Business Profile, industry directories)
- Guest posts and cross links with peers
- Open source projects or public tools that earn natural links
- Substack/newsletter cross linking back to portfolio domain

**96.98% of desktop clicks go to page one results.** Without backlinks, a new portfolio domain will struggle to reach page one for competitive terms.

Source: [Wix: Portfolio SEO Guide](https://www.wix.com/blog/portfolio-seo)

### 7. Platform Choice and SEO

If organic search traffic is a primary goal, the platform hierarchy based on this research:

1. **Webflow** (best SEO control, cleanest output, best performance)
2. **Custom site** (full control but requires maintenance)
3. **Super.so** (surprisingly good SEO for a Notion wrapper; proper meta tags, CDN, sitemaps)
4. **Squarespace** (solid defaults, less customizable)
5. **Framer** (good but slightly behind Webflow on performance metrics)
6. **Wix** (decent but code bloat hurts speed scores)
7. **Platform profiles** (Contra, Polywork) offer near zero SEO autonomy
