# Portfolio Website Issues That Burn Candidates

Ranked by frequency across sources. Research conducted October 2026.

---

## 1. No Evidence of Impact (Missing Metrics and Outcomes)

**Frequency signal:** Mentioned in 7 of 8 sources. The single most repeated complaint across hiring managers, career coaches, and portfolio reviewers.

**Description:** Portfolios that showcase deliverables, process steps, or pretty visuals without showing measurable business results. Hiring managers see wireframes and journey maps but no proof anything moved a number. This is the gap that separates "looks like they do work" from "looks like they drive results."

**Verbatim quotes:**
- "If you **can** achieve the results, it's **expected** you know the process. If you **can't** achieve the results, it **doesn't matter** how much process you know!" -- Learn UI Design
- "Numbers transform your portfolio from a gallery into evidence. Without them, you're just showing pretty pictures with no proof they actually worked." -- The Interview Guys
- "No business impact data... Vague impact statements" -- UX Playbook

**Sources:**
- https://www.learnui.design/blog/portfolio-mistakes.html
- https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/how-to-make-a-portfolio-website-that-gets-you-hired/
- https://uxplaybook.org/articles/11-common-ux-portfolio-mistakes-and-solutions
- https://designlab.com/blog/ux-portfolio-mistakes-to-avoid
- https://www.joinleland.com/library/a/product-manager-portfolio

**Takeaway:** Every project entry needs at least one hard number showing business impact; process documentation without outcomes is filler.

## 2. Walls of Text / Poor Readability

**Frequency signal:** Mentioned in 6 of 8 sources.

**Description:** Long, unbroken paragraphs that force readers to wade through prose instead of scanning for signal. Users absorb only 20 to 28 percent of web page content. Hiring managers reviewing dozens of portfolios will not read essay length case studies.

**Verbatim quotes:**
- "3-5 sentences is usually sufficient for each paragraph." -- Designlab
- "Users scan rather than read websites, absorbing only 20-28% of content." -- WebGen World

**Sources:**
- https://www.learnui.design/blog/portfolio-mistakes.html
- https://designlab.com/blog/ux-portfolio-mistakes-to-avoid
- https://uxplaybook.org/articles/11-common-ux-portfolio-mistakes-and-solutions
- https://webgenworld.com/blog/most-common-problems-in-website-design/
- https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/how-to-make-a-portfolio-website-that-gets-you-hired/

**Takeaway:** Replace long paragraphs with scannable elements: bullet lists, bold key phrases, annotated images, and pull out stats.

---

## 3. Missing or Buried Contact Info / No Clear CTA

**Frequency signal:** Mentioned in 6 of 8 sources.

**Description:** Visitors who want to reach out cannot find contact information or a next step. The email is hidden in a footer, behind a nav link, or absent entirely. 70 percent of small business websites lack a clear CTA on their homepage; portfolio sites are equally guilty.

**Verbatim quotes:**
- "If a hiring manager loves your work but can't easily figure out how to reach you, that enthusiasm dies fast." -- The Interview Guys
- "Portfolios where the contact email is hidden in a footer or behind a navigation link silently lose interviews." -- UX Companion
- "Pages with single, clear CTAs can increase conversions by up to 371%." -- WebGen World

**Sources:**
- https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/how-to-make-a-portfolio-website-that-gets-you-hired/
- https://uxplaybook.org/articles/11-common-ux-portfolio-mistakes-and-solutions
- https://designlab.com/blog/ux-portfolio-mistakes-to-avoid
- https://webgenworld.com/blog/most-common-problems-in-website-design/

**Takeaway:** Contact info and a clear CTA ("Let's talk" / "Book a call") must be visible on every page, not just buried in a footer.

---

## 4. Not Mobile Responsive

**Frequency signal:** Mentioned in 5 of 8 sources.

**Description:** Portfolios that break, crop, or become unusable on phones. Over 60 percent of web traffic comes from mobile devices, and recruiters regularly review portfolios on their phones between meetings. A non responsive portfolio tells a reviewer you do not test your own work.

**Verbatim quotes:**
- "If your portfolio breaks on a phone, you're turning away more than half of potential visitors." -- The Interview Guys
- "61% of users won't return to a mobile site they had trouble accessing." -- WebGen World
- "Mobile users are 5x more likely to abandon tasks on non-optimized sites." -- WebGen World

**Sources:**
- https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/how-to-make-a-portfolio-website-that-gets-you-hired/
- https://webgenworld.com/blog/most-common-problems-in-website-design/
- https://designlab.com/blog/ux-portfolio-mistakes-to-avoid
- https://hakia.com/skills/building-portfolio/

**Takeaway:** Test on real phones, not just browser dev tools. If a recruiter opens it on an iPhone between meetings and it is broken, you are out.

## 5. Poor Visual Design / Weak First Impression

**Frequency signal:** Mentioned in 5 of 8 sources.

**Description:** Bland aesthetics, low resolution images, inconsistent visuals, and generic stock photography. Hiring managers form snap judgments within seconds. A portfolio that looks amateurish undermines every claim of competence on the page.

**Verbatim quotes:**
- "If you can't make your own dang portfolio look good, hiring managers will (rightly) think twice about it!" -- Learn UI Design
- "Cluttered with bad grammar, inconsistent visuals, and low-resolution images." -- Dribbble
- "Sadly, they're everywhere. They're in at least 70% of portfolios I've seen." (re: low fidelity images) -- Dribbble

**Sources:**
- https://www.learnui.design/blog/portfolio-mistakes.html
- https://dribbble.com/stories/2021/05/18/5-design-portfolio-mistakes-that-ll-send-clients-running-in-the-wrong-direction
- https://uxplaybook.org/articles/11-common-ux-portfolio-mistakes-and-solutions
- https://designlab.com/blog/ux-portfolio-mistakes-to-avoid

**Takeaway:** Clean typography, high resolution images, consistent spacing, and intentional color use. Does not need to be flashy; needs to look deliberate.

## 6. Slow Loading / Heavy Assets

**Frequency signal:** Mentioned in 4 of 8 sources.

**Description:** Large unoptimized images, heavy animations, and bloated frameworks push load times past the threshold where visitors leave. 47 percent of users expect pages to load in 2 seconds or less. Pages loading in 5+ seconds see 90 percent higher bounce rates.

**Verbatim quotes:**
- "If yours takes more than a few seconds to load, they'll move on." -- The Interview Guys
- "Pages loading in 5+ seconds have 90% higher bounce rates." -- WebGen World
- One case study showed bounce rate dropped from 75% to 35% after load time went from 8 seconds to 2.1 seconds. -- WebGen World

**Sources:**
- https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/how-to-make-a-portfolio-website-that-gets-you-hired/
- https://webgenworld.com/blog/most-common-problems-in-website-design/
- https://uxplaybook.org/articles/11-common-ux-portfolio-mistakes-and-solutions

**Takeaway:** Compress images, lazy load below the fold content, and test with Google PageSpeed Insights. Target under 3 seconds on mobile.

---

## 7. Broken Links / Poor Maintenance

**Frequency signal:** Mentioned in 4 of 8 sources.

**Description:** Dead links, outdated project references, and 404 errors signal neglect. Hiring managers have specifically called out "broken links in portfolios that were only a few weeks or months old."

**Verbatim quotes:**
- "Websites have to be maintained... broken links in portfolios that were only a few weeks or months old." -- Tiago Dionesto Willrich da Silva, Engineering Team Lead (via Dev.to survey)
- "Broken links increase bounce rates by 25% and signal poor maintenance to search engines." -- WebGen World

**Sources:**
- https://dev.to/profydev/this-survey-among-60-hiring-managers-reveals-don-t-waste-your-time-on-a-react-portfolio-website-17ge
- https://webgenworld.com/blog/most-common-problems-in-website-design/
- https://uxplaybook.org/articles/11-common-ux-portfolio-mistakes-and-solutions

**Takeaway:** Audit every link quarterly. Use a link checker tool before any job application push.

---

## 8. Too Much Style, Not Enough Substance (Fluff and Buzzwords)

**Frequency signal:** Mentioned in 4 of 8 sources.

**Description:** Portfolios heavy on design flourishes, generic "I'm a passionate problem solver" copy, and corporate buzzwords but light on concrete work evidence. Reviewers see through it fast.

**Verbatim quotes:**
- "Overuse of buzzwords... Vague descriptions of work... Empty claims without substance." -- UX Playbook
- "Dropping in a beautiful final design without explaining the problem it solved is like showing the last scene of a movie without the rest of the plot." -- The Interview Guys
- "Websites are cool and look nice but they open the door for more interpretation and criticism because they'd naturally form an opinion." -- Louis H., Recruiter (via Dev.to survey)

**Sources:**
- https://uxplaybook.org/articles/11-common-ux-portfolio-mistakes-and-solutions
- https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/how-to-make-a-portfolio-website-that-gets-you-hired/
- https://dev.to/profydev/this-survey-among-60-hiring-managers-reveals-don-t-waste-your-time-on-a-react-portfolio-website-17ge
- https://dribbble.com/stories/2021/05/18/5-design-portfolio-mistakes-that-ll-send-clients-running-in-the-wrong-direction

**Takeaway:** Every visual choice and piece of copy should serve a purpose. If it does not explain what you did, why, or what happened as a result, cut it.
