# Who Would Visit and Engage: Visitor Segment Analysis

Research compiled October 13, 2026. All findings sourced; unsourced claims excluded.

---

## 1. Recruiters (Agency and In-House)

**Evidence of visit behavior:** 82% of recruiters check at least one external link before scheduling an interview (Profy.dev survey of 300+ recruiters). CareerBuilder's survey found 70% of employers use social media and online search to screen applicants, up 18 percentage points in two years. 47% of employers say if they cannot find a candidate online, they are less likely to call them for an interview.

**Frequency signals:** Visit happens once per candidate, during the screening stage (post application, pre interview). Recruiters spend 3 to 5 minutes skimming a portfolio site. The window is narrow: most screening decisions land within 48 to 72 hours of initial review.

**Willingness to engage:** High for passive outreach (sending InMail, scheduling a screen) if the site confirms qualifications. 61% of employers researching candidates online are specifically looking for information that supports qualifications. Only 15% of candidates provide a portfolio link, so having one creates immediate differentiation.

**Source URLs:**
- [Myseera: Do You Need a Personal Website in 2026?](https://myseera.com/blog/personal-website-for-job-seekers) (cites Profy.dev survey)
- [CareerBuilder: 7 in 10 Employers Research Candidates Online](https://www.careerbuilder.com/advice/employer-blog/employers-research-candidate-social-media-profiles)

**Takeaway:** Recruiters are the highest frequency single visit segment; the portfolio exists to pass their 3 to 5 minute screening filter, not to bring them back.

## 2. Hiring Managers

**Evidence of visit behavior:** 93% of hiring managers would examine a candidate's portfolio if provided (Profy.dev survey of 300+ recruiters and hiring managers). 71% of employers say portfolio quality directly influences their hiring decision (Hover survey of 121 hiring managers). 56% of hiring managers said a personal website impressed them more than any other personal branding tool. Multiple hiring managers at The Muse confirmed they have "absolutely" hired candidates because of exceptional personal sites, with team members referencing the candidate's website in one on one conversations.

**What they look for:** Projects and process (what was the idea, how were decisions made, what was the outcome), a clear value proposition bio (2 to 5 lines), and evidence of thinking rather than just claims. One Eventbrite hiring manager said: "I'd feel much more comfortable contacting someone when I know how you think and what I can expect from working with you." A failed project with honest analysis impressed hiring managers more than polished success stories.

**Frequency signals:** Visits at two points: shortlisting (pre phone screen) and pre final interview. May revisit to prep interview questions or share the link with other interviewers on the panel.

**Willingness to engage:** Very high. A resume is a claim; a website is evidence. The site directly shapes interview questions and confidence in extending an offer.

**Source URLs:**
- [Myseera: Do You Need a Personal Website in 2026?](https://myseera.com/blog/personal-website-for-job-seekers)
- [The Muse: Can a Personal Website Help Your Job Search?](https://www.themuse.com/advice/can-a-personal-website-help-your-job-search-what-6-hiring-managers-really-think)

**Takeaway:** Hiring managers are the highest value visitor segment. They visit with intent to evaluate, they spend more time than recruiters, and the site directly influences the hire/no hire decision.

## 3. PE/VC Partners and Portfolio Operators

**Evidence of visit behavior:** Investors and PE partners Google candidates before the first meeting, not after. The vetting shapes initial impressions before any conversation begins. A founder or executive whose search results yield only LinkedIn and a company website signals insufficient credibility cultivation, which "removes leverage during investor triage." Later stage investors (Series B/C, growth equity) weight public records more heavily due to formal diligence protocols and analyst involvement.

**What they look for:** Independent validation of claimed traction over self published content. Three genuine press features outperform fifty self published posts because external editorial filters signal credibility. They assess consistency of public coverage, evidence of ability to articulate company narrative externally, and independent validation of claimed traction.

**Frequency signals:** Visit once during candidate assessment, but the assessment is deeper than recruiter screening. PE executive search is a 12 step process involving retained search firms that perform extensive market research. The portfolio site is one node in a broader research sweep.

**Willingness to engage:** Moderate to high, but engagement is mediated through executive search firms (Spencer Stuart, Russell Reynolds, Christian and Timbers). Direct outreach from PE partners is rare; the portfolio site influences the search firm's recommendation, which then influences the partner.

**Source URLs:**
- [We Live to Build: What Investors Look For When They Google You](https://www.welivetobuild.com/blog/what-investors-look-for-when-they-google-you)
- [Heidrick & Struggles: PE Operating Company CEO Hiring](https://www.heidrick.com/en/pages/private-equity/the-landscape-for-hiring-a-pe-operating-company-ceo-is-changing-fast)
- [Alpha Apex Group: Top PE Executive Search Firms 2026](https://www.alphaapexgroup.com/blog/top-private-equity-executive-search-firms)

**Takeaway:** PE/VC visitors are low frequency but extremely high stakes. The portfolio is part of a due diligence package; it needs to show P&L ownership, board level communication, and value creation outcomes rather than design polish.

## 4. Potential Co-Founders and Business Partners

**Evidence of visit behavior:** Due diligence on potential partners includes Google searches with specific queries (name + lawsuit, name + company, name + track record). Partners evaluate business ownership history, reputation, track record of reliability, and past projects. One source states: "By the time you're in the room, the associate who prepped the partner has already skimmed whatever came up, which means the meeting starts with an impression already partially formed."

**Frequency signals:** Visit once during evaluation, before the first meeting. May return during deeper due diligence if partnership advances.

**Willingness to engage:** High if the site establishes credibility. The site's role is to be the controlled first page result that answers the question "who is this person and can I trust them with a business relationship."

**Source URLs:**
- [Online Reputation: Why Buyers, Partners, and Investors Are Googling You](https://www.onlinereputation.com/why-buyers-partners-and-investors-are-googling-you-before-they-ever-reach-out/)
- [Smart Passive Income: Guide to Finding Business Partners](https://www.smartpassiveincome.com/blog/guide-to-finding-business-partners/)
- [Forecastr: Tips to Finding and Vetting Co-Founders](https://forecastr.com/blog/tips-to-finding-and-vetting-co-founders)

**Takeaway:** Similar profile to PE visitors but with more personal assessment. The portfolio must answer "can I trust this person with shared equity and shared risk."

## 5. Content Audience (Substack and LinkedIn Cross-Traffic)

**Evidence of visit behavior:** Substack structurally discourages cross traffic to external sites; all traffic from Substack recommendations bypasses the author's website. A generic link in a Substack newsletter generates minimal clicks. However, LinkedIn thought leadership content performs differently: decision makers spend more than one hour per week consuming thought leadership (Edelman LinkedIn 2025 study), and approximately 54% of decision makers research new offerings after encountering thought leadership content.

**Frequency signals:** LinkedIn to portfolio cross traffic is recurring but low conversion. CEO posts generate 7x more impressions than company page posts, and C suite content gets 5x more views than other LinkedIn members (Inc.). But click through from LinkedIn post to external site is typically single digit percentage of impressions.

**Willingness to engage:** Moderate. 55% of decision makers use thought leadership content to evaluate potential vendors or partners (Edelman LinkedIn 2025). The path is: see LinkedIn post, click profile, see portfolio link, visit site. Each step loses most of the audience.

**Source URLs:**
- [Charlotte Duckworth: Why Substack Is Not a Substitute for Your Author Website](https://www.charlotteduckworthstudio.com/blog/substack-author-website)
- [Phantom IQ: Thought Leadership Statistics 2026](https://phantom-iq.com/thought-leadership-statistics) (cites Edelman LinkedIn 2025 and Inc.)
- [Inc.: Executives Who Post on LinkedIn Get 3x More Leads](https://www.inc.com/nicole-ramirez/executives-who-post-on-linkedin-get-3x-more-leads-heres-why-brand-pages-cant-compete/91339459)

**Takeaway:** LinkedIn is the primary funnel for content driven portfolio visits, not Substack. The portfolio must be linked prominently from the LinkedIn profile, not buried in newsletter footers.

## 6. Peers and Industry Connections

**Evidence of visit behavior:** No direct survey data found on how often peers visit a colleague's portfolio site. However, the Edelman LinkedIn 2025 study shows 79% of decision makers say they are more likely to advocate for someone with strong thought leadership, and 64% of buyers say thought leadership content is more trustworthy than marketing materials for assessing capabilities. In hiring contexts, multiple team members at one company referenced a candidate's website during one on one conversations (The Muse), suggesting peer sharing of portfolio links happens organically during evaluation.

**Frequency signals:** Sporadic. Triggered by job changes, conference appearances, viral posts, or referral requests ("do you know someone for this role?").

**Willingness to engage:** Low to moderate for cold visits, but high when referred. The portfolio's role for peers is to make referrals easy: "here, look at this person's site" is more convincing than forwarding a resume.

**Source URLs:**
- [Phantom IQ: Thought Leadership Statistics 2026](https://phantom-iq.com/thought-leadership-statistics) (cites Edelman LinkedIn 2025)
- [The Muse: Can a Personal Website Help Your Job Search?](https://www.themuse.com/advice/can-a-personal-website-help-your-job-search-what-6-hiring-managers-really-think)

**Takeaway:** Peers are the least frequent visitors but the most powerful amplifiers. The site must be shareable; a compelling case study page that a peer can forward is worth more than a polished homepage.

| Rank | Segment | Visit Likelihood | Visit Frequency | Value per Visit | Priority |

| 1 | Hiring Managers | Very High (93% would look) | 1 to 2 visits per hiring cycle | Highest (directly influences offer) | Build for this audience first |

| 3 | PE/VC Partners + Search Firms | Moderate (Google before meeting) | 1 visit per assessment | Very High (career defining) | Ensure site ranks on page 1 for name search |

| 6 | Peers/Network | Low | Sporadic | Low individually, high via referral | Make case studies shareable |

**Design implication:** The site's primary job is to convert a hiring manager's 5 to 10 minute evaluation visit into interview confidence. Secondary job: appear authoritative in Google results for Aju Joseph John so PE search firms and potential partners see a controlled narrative.

## What I Could Not Find

- **Exact traffic source breakdowns** from real portfolio site analytics (e.g., "40% direct, 30% LinkedIn, 20% Google"). No one has published granular Google Analytics data for a senior executive portfolio site.
- **PE specific candidate vetting statistics.** PE firms and executive search firms do not publish data on how often they visit candidate personal websites vs. relying on LinkedIn or proprietary databases.
- **Reddit or Blind discussion threads with usable data.** Blind's post on "do FAANG recruiters look at your personal website" was not renderable; Reddit threads on this topic returned generic Wikipedia results rather than discussion content.
- **Substack to external site click through rates.** Substack does not publish cross traffic data, and no Substack author has published portfolio site referral traffic numbers.
- **Canada specific recruiter behavior data.** All surveys found (CareerBuilder, Profy.dev, Hover) are US centric. No equivalent Canadian data was located.
- **Frequency of return visits** by any segment. All data found addresses "do they visit" not "do they come back."
