# ADR 0002: Visitor #1 Is Hiring Managers

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-13

## Decision

The portfolio's information architecture is optimized for hiring managers as the primary visitor. PE/VC search firms, recruiters, co-founders, and peers benefit secondarily.

## Alternatives

1. **Optimize for PE/VC search firms.** Scannable metrics dashboard, P&L proof points above the fold, minimal narrative. Faster to scan but does not show thinking quality, which is the differentiator at the VP/GM/CEO level.
2. **Try to serve all six segments equally.** Dilutes the page structure. A page optimized for a 90 second scan and a 7 minute deep read is optimized for neither.

## Why

- 93% of hiring managers would examine a portfolio if provided (research/summary.md, Section 5).
- Hiring managers spend 5 to 10 minutes and may revisit before final interviews. They evaluate decision narratives, trade-offs, and evidence of thinking.
- Decision narratives that convert a hiring manager also satisfy a PE/VC search firm (depth is a bonus for scanners), but a scannable metrics dashboard does not show thinking quality. The reverse does not hold.
- The portfolio's job is to convert people who are already interested enough to click through. Hiring managers are that audience.

## Consequences

- Case studies are written for depth (decision narrative structure: context, problem, decision, execution, outcome, learning), not for scanning.
- The homepage leads with the value proposition and featured case studies, not a metrics dashboard.
- PE/VC search firms get what they need (P&L numbers, board governance, scale) but embedded within narratives, not pulled out into a separate summary view. This may cost 30 extra seconds of their time.
- Recruiters are not the primary audience. The portfolio is not a resume substitute; it is a credibility deepener for people who already have the resume.
