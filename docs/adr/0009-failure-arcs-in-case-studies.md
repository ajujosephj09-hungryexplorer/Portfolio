# ADR 0009: Case Studies Include Failure Arcs

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-13

## Decision

At least two of the four V1 case studies include a visible failure or "I was wrong" beat. Not self-deprecation. Judgment under uncertainty, where a decision went wrong, the consequences were real, and the recovery required a different kind of thinking.

The Ensemble case study (Case Study 1) will include the CH1→CH2→CH3 arc from the story bank:

1. **CH1 (Trial Extension Failure):** Extended trial from 2 to 4 weeks despite sales pushback. PLG conversion dropped from 25% to 10%. Revenue fell 40% below target. Sales had warned that buying is impulsive; they were right.
2. **CH2 (Sales Recovery):** Flipped trial back, offered targeted discounts, cleaned 20%+ of stale pipeline, replenished with better leads. Recovered to 140% of next quarter target.
3. **CH3 (Unit Economics Fix):** The recovery discounts crashed LTV:CAC from 4:1 to 1.3:1. Fixed by improving product value first, then raising prices, adding engagement plays, and investing in PLG funnel.

This is a three-part arc where each fix creates the next problem. It shows learning velocity and systems thinking, not just a one-time mistake.

The AI Sales Agent case study (Case Study 2) should also include a failure beat if one exists (rep resistance, bad early recommendations, scope creep). Source material TBD.

## Alternatives

1. **Keep case studies clean.** Only show the positive arc. The research explicitly warns against this: "A portfolio with zero visible friction anywhere doesn't read as accomplished. It reads as edited." (research/summary.md, Challenge 3, Emily Backes quote)
2. **Add a separate "Lessons Learned" section.** Isolates failures from the main narrative. Creates a section that reads as performative humility rather than integrated judgment.

## Why

- Hiring managers for senior roles specifically look for judgment under constraint (research/summary.md, Challenge 3).
- The CH1→CH2→CH3 arc is one of the strongest stories in the bank because it is not one mistake but a cascading sequence. It demonstrates that Aju can diagnose second-order effects, not just first-order problems.
- A VP at a Series C startup said the decision narrative (not polish) is what converted him from evaluator to recruiter (research/summary.md, Challenge 3).

## Consequences

- The Ensemble case study becomes longer and more complex. This is acceptable because it is the flagship story (0 to 1 incubation is the rarest capability).
- The decision narrative structure must accommodate the failure arc naturally: context → decision → failure → diagnosis → recovery → second-order failure → fix → outcome.
- The failure must be specific (numbers, timeline, what went wrong) not vague ("we had some setbacks").
