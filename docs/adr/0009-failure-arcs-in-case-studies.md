# ADR 0009: Case Studies Include Failure Arcs

**Status:** Accepted (revised 2026-10-13)
**Date:** 2026-10-13

## Decision

At least one V1 case study includes a deep, multi-beat failure arc. Not self-deprecation. Judgment under uncertainty, where a decision went wrong, the consequences were real, and the recovery required a different kind of thinking.

The Ensemble case study carries the CH1→CH2→CH3 arc from the story bank:

1. **CH1 (Trial Extension Failure):** Extended trial from 2 to 4 weeks despite sales pushback. PLG conversion dropped from 25% to 10%. Revenue fell 40% below target. Sales had warned that buying is impulsive; they were right.
2. **CH2 (Sales Recovery):** Flipped trial back, offered targeted discounts, cleaned 20%+ of stale pipeline, replenished with better leads. Recovered to 140% of next quarter target.
3. **CH3 (Unit Economics Fix):** The recovery discounts crashed LTV:CAC from 4:1 to 1.3:1. Fixed by improving product value first, then raising prices, adding engagement plays, and investing in PLG funnel.

This is a three-part arc where each fix creates the next problem. It shows learning velocity and systems thinking, not just a one-time mistake.

No other case study is required to include a forced failure arc. The CH10 Turnaround, HPCC, and Redline stories are strong without one. Including failure for the sake of failure is not worth it.

## Alternatives

1. **Require two failure arcs.** Original decision. Rejected because no natural failure beat exists in the other three case studies. Forcing one would be artificial and undercut authenticity.
2. **Keep case studies clean.** Only show the positive arc. The research explicitly warns against this: "A portfolio with zero visible friction anywhere doesn't read as accomplished. It reads as edited." (research/summary.md, Challenge 3, Emily Backes quote)
3. **Add a separate "Lessons Learned" section.** Isolates failures from the main narrative. Creates a section that reads as performative humility rather than integrated judgment.

## Why

- Hiring managers for senior roles specifically look for judgment under constraint (research/summary.md, Challenge 3).
- The CH1→CH2→CH3 arc is one of the strongest stories in the bank because it is not one mistake but a cascading sequence. It demonstrates that Aju can diagnose second-order effects, not just first-order problems.
- One deep, authentic failure arc is more powerful than two thin ones. The three-part cascade in Ensemble is rich enough to carry the portfolio's credibility on this axis.
- A VP at a Series C startup said the decision narrative (not polish) is what converted him from evaluator to recruiter (research/summary.md, Challenge 3).

## Consequences

- The Ensemble case study becomes longer and more complex. This is acceptable because it is the 0 to 1 incubation story.
- The decision narrative structure must accommodate the failure arc naturally: context → decision → failure → diagnosis → recovery → second-order failure → fix → outcome.
- The failure must be specific (numbers, timeline, what went wrong) not vague ("we had some setbacks").
- Other case studies may include natural friction points if the source material supports them, but they are not required to.
