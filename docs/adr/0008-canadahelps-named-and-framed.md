# ADR 0008: CanadaHelps Named and Framed, Not Buried

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-13

## Decision

CanadaHelps is named in case study titles and framed with commercial scale in the same breath. The site does not minimize, bury, or delay the name. Format: "CanadaHelps: Canada's largest online giving and payments platform. $260M+ processing volume. $27M+ P&L."

## Alternatives

1. **Lead with metrics, name CanadaHelps later.** Avoids the nonprofit snap judgment but creates a different problem: a smart interviewer will Google the company, see it is a nonprofit, and wonder why you downplayed it. Appearing to hide it is worse than naming it.
2. **Use only "B2B SaaS & Payments" without the company name.** Removes the snap judgment entirely but reads as evasive. Anonymous case studies signal that you cannot or will not claim the work publicly.

## Why

- The research says polished perfection signals inauthenticity at the executive level (research/summary.md, Challenge 3). Hiding the employer name is a form of over-polishing.
- Controlling the framing is better than ceding it. If the reader discovers the name themselves, they attach their own framing. If you name it and frame it, you control the first impression.
- The commercial metrics ($260M+ processing, $27M+ P&L, 200+ SaaS customers, 45pp conversion lift) are the reframe. The name is not the problem; the absence of numbers would be.

## Consequences

- Every CanadaHelps case study leads with the company name and commercial scale.
- The About page career timeline names CanadaHelps with the same framing.
- No visitor should encounter the name without immediately seeing the commercial context.
